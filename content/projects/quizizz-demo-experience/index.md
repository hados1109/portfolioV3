# Process

<h1>Prequel</h1>
<p>In the months prior to this, we had experimented extensively around user activation. Our top contenders were:</p>
<ol><li>Reverse trials</li><li>Referral programs (x3)</li><li>Mailers and in-product nudges towards activation</li></ol>
<p>&nbsp;</p>
<p>None of these seemed to work as expected and we were trying to give one final boost to our activation metrics before the back-to-school season.</p>
<p>&nbsp;</p>
<h1>Hypothesis</h1>
<p>In the midst of this emerged an idea, that we are maybe not communicating our features well enough.</p>
<p>&nbsp;</p>
<p>Onboarding emails, banners, and pop-ups are easily ignored. Additionally, there was no onboarding on our product. This is where a new user lands after signing up:</p>
<p>&nbsp;</p>
<figure class="figure-image align-center"><div><img alt="" src="screenshot-2025-03-09-at-7-04-50-pm.avif"></div></figure>
<p>&nbsp;</p>
<p>Hence, it is safe to assume that we did not do a good enough job with onboarding new users. Without any education on how to use the product, users saw us as any generic presentation or quiz-taking tool, and quickly dropped off. The value that Quizizz provides was only visible to long-term users.</p>
<p>&nbsp;</p>
<p>A hypothesis formed up:</p>
<blockquote>A new user does not understand the value that Quizizz provides over others due to our weak feature communication. If they saw value in Quizizz, they would start using it more actively.</blockquote>
<p>&nbsp;</p>
<h1>What's different about Quizizz?</h1>
<p>Quizizz offers a diverse set of services:</p>
<ol><li>Millions of free, ready-to-host resources</li><li>Different formats like assessment, lesson, interactive video, flashcard and passage</li><li>AI-enabled creation and report generation</li><li>17+ question types like none other, including hotspot, categorise, comprehension, draw, graphing, and much more</li><li>Student accommodations for the differently abled</li><li>Student-loved engagement features such as themes and coins economy</li><li>Standards and curriculum aligned resource library</li><li>LMS integrations, and much more</li></ol>
<p>&nbsp;</p>
<p>The idea was to educate a new user about all these features in the hopes that they would retain and activate.</p>
<p>&nbsp;</p>
<h1>How can we showcase these features?</h1>
<p>The leading idea was to showcase this in the first time experience post signup. Let's see how it evolved over time.</p>
<p>&nbsp;</p>
<h3>Attempt 1 - Newbie-friendly homepage</h3>
<p>The explore page (home page) did not have any cues as to what a new user should do. It only suggested activities to host and topics to teach. These were high commitment actions and users were hesitant to enter the funnel.</p>
<p>&nbsp;</p>
<p>What if we could surface some low-commitment actions? These could include explainer videos, exploratory features, or just a disclaimer of all the features that are available at their disposal.</p>
<p>&nbsp;</p>
<figure class="figure-video align-center" style="padding-bottom:33.723653395784545%"><div><iframe src="https://www.youtube.com/embed/udTwle2cCLw" title="Quizizz Demo - Attempt 1" allowfullscreen></iframe></div></figure>
<p>&nbsp;</p>
<p>We hoped that with these numerous short actions, explainer videos, and guided tours, a user would find value in some feature and would activate in that flow.</p>
<p>&nbsp;</p>
<p>Although the idea was exciting, we quickly realised the problem.</p>
<p>&nbsp;</p>
<h3>Do we need to communicate everything?</h3>
<p>The initial feedback was that it is too overwhelming. We were trying to communicate everything in one go, with equal weightage to everything. Maybe this was too much to consume for a new user.</p>
<p>&nbsp;</p>
<p>Let's revisit our list of features again:</p>
<ol><li>Millions of free resources</li><li>Different formats</li><li>AI-enabled practice</li><li>17+ question types</li><li>Student accommodations</li><li>Student-loved engagement features</li><li>Standards and curriculum aligned library</li><li>LMS integrations</li></ol>
<p>&nbsp;</p>
<p>Out of these:</p>
<ul><li>1-2 were more generic rather than being USPs</li><li>3-5 were teacher-centric and were our top USPs that teachers directly empathised with</li><li>6 was more student-focused</li><li>7-8 helped teachers, but were good-to-have features and not necessarily game-changers</li></ul>
<p>Hence, we decided to prioritise our top 3 features (points 3-5) and try to educate our users about these first.</p>
<p>&nbsp;</p>
<blockquote>Our bet now lied in our top 3 features: diverse question types, AI-powered teaching, and student accommodations.</blockquote>
<p>&nbsp;</p>
<h3>Attempt 2 - Immersive signup experience</h3>
<p>Along with the content, teachers were also interested in how the content would be shown, i.e. the gameplay experience. The medium presentation mattered to the teacher. This was only a small part of the last version, while it was a leading factor in piquing users' interest.</p>
<p>&nbsp;</p>
<p>The next idea was to give the teacher a taste of the gameplay experience in the signup process itself.</p>
<p>&nbsp;</p>
<figure class="figure-video align-center" style="padding-bottom:33.723653395784545%"><div><iframe src="https://www.youtube.com/embed/geEthQo1JPo" title="Quizizz Demo - Attempt 2" allowfullscreen></iframe></div></figure>
<p>&nbsp;</p>
<p>Here the teacher would complete a part of their profile that they usually did during the signup. By utilising different question types, we can create an onboarding experience that gives you a first-hand experience of the final deliverable.</p>
<p>&nbsp;</p>
<p>One their profile is complete and they know what the audience will see, we can carry on the experience to show how our 3 USPs are enhance the experience of the audience.</p>
<p>&nbsp;</p>
<p>This attempt had some complications:</p>
<ul><li>Developers had to create a custom game link every time</li><li>Possible inaccuracies in subject selection</li><li>Not all question types were optimised for mobile and tablet view</li></ul>
<p>&nbsp;</p>
<p>Along with this, we were also concerned about drop-offs due to the additional effort and non-conventional signup forms.</p>
<p>&nbsp;</p>
<p>Maybe we could complete the signup normally and then focus on the feature ed.</p>
<p>&nbsp;</p>
<h3>Attempt 3 - Post-signup feature education</h3>
<p>In this attempt, the signup was completed normally. The demo happened on the first time interaction with the product.</p>
<p>&nbsp;</p>
<figure class="figure-video align-center" style="padding-bottom:33.723653395784545%"><div><iframe src="https://www.youtube.com/embed/3sjwLtIF5jc" title="Quizizz Demo - Attempt 3" allowfullscreen></iframe></div></figure>
<p>&nbsp;</p>
<p>This was obviously a step-up because of two reasons:</p>
<ul><li>The signup process wasn't hindered and we could collect clean data easily</li><li>The demo could be more personalised since we knew their subject and grade</li></ul>
<p>&nbsp;</p>
<p>The end state was also changed to make it more search-first, that being the easiest action.</p>
<p>&nbsp;</p>
<p>With lesser commitment in their actions, the teacher could explore the platform freely and without distractions.</p>
<p>&nbsp;</p>
<p>In the midst of this emerged another idea:</p>
<p>&nbsp;</p>
<h3>Can value communication happen pre-signup?</h3>
<p>The signup completion rate was close to 60%. This meant that around 40% of users will never get to see the in-product value with our experience, and some of the 60% cohort might even skip or drop-off during the onboarding, reducing the number even more.</p>
<p>&nbsp;</p>
<p>Maybe if we had this experience pre-signup, all users could experience the value that Quizizz provides in a more non-invasive and less-committing way.</p>
<p>&nbsp;</p>
<h3>Attempt 4 - Pre-signup video-led gameplay</h3>
<p>In this attempt, we changed the CTA on the website from 'Sign up' to 'Try it now'.</p>
<p>&nbsp;</p>
<figure class="figure-image align-center"><div><img alt="" src="frame-26086216.avif"></div></figure>
<p>&nbsp;</p>
<p>This came with some possible risks:</p>
<ul><li>Reduced signups</li><li>Confusion regarding where can a user signup</li></ul>
<p>&nbsp;</p>
<p>An alternative touchpoint for the same was suggested.&nbsp;This would surface as its own separate section on the website to preserve the signup button:</p>
<p>&nbsp;</p>
<figure class="figure-image align-center"><div><img alt="" src="frame-26086219.avif"></div></figure>
<p>&nbsp;</p>
<p>Contrary to popular opinion, we wanted to try out replacing the signup button in a more aggressive approach. For the sake of this experiment, we were ready to tank the signup numbers in the lesser-traffic months to have stronger activations in the back-to-schools season.</p>
<p>&nbsp;</p>
<p>We also wanted a better medium for education that can show our features in a more expressive and interactive manner. For this we utilised our interactive video feature.</p>
<p>&nbsp;</p>
<p>In this, the user would watch an explainer video with some follow-up questions in between. This video would be a Quizizz explainer, while in the questions we can ask them how they think that feature could help them in their practice.</p>
<p>&nbsp;</p>
<p>This is how it turned up:</p>
<p>&nbsp;</p>
<figure class="figure-video align-center" style="padding-bottom:33.723653395784545%"><div><iframe src="https://www.youtube.com/embed/T7W9PykguDc" title="Quizizz Demo - Attempt 4" allowfullscreen></iframe></div></figure>
<p>&nbsp;</p>
<p>The idea seemed good. There's one issue we faced though. With such an aggressive demo option, we left out the personalisation bit. Now the entire demo was generic and Quizizz centric.</p>
<p>&nbsp;</p>
<p>Maybe there was some hope to make this thing a little more custom, a little more personal.</p>
<p>&nbsp;</p>
<h3>Attempt 5 - Bringing customisation back</h3>
<p>The previous idea was tweaked to add a bit of personalisation to the experience.</p>
<p>&nbsp;</p>
<p>The first step now involved asking the user their subject and grade, and the subsequent interactive video would be tailored to these parameters, plus a few others like location and language.</p>
<p>&nbsp;</p>
<figure class="figure-video align-center" style="padding-bottom:33.723653395784545%"><div><iframe src="https://www.youtube.com/embed/tM-9QjX99ZU" title="Quizizz Demo - Attempt 5" allowfullscreen></iframe></div></figure>
<p>&nbsp;</p>
<p>This seemed a good experience, with minimal effort and maximal value communication.</p>
<p>&nbsp;</p>
<p>This experience seemed amazing, aligned with all our goals and communicated value in a delightful manner. But we asked ourselves an important question now: Does every teacher necessarily need to experience the demo?</p>
<p>&nbsp;</p>
<p>Most teachers get to know about Quizizz via word of mouth. They share the features and gamification of Quizizz in their staff rooms and over lunch. While sharing teachers primarily showed each other the gameplay experience.</p>
<p>&nbsp;</p>
<p>If we assume that a majority of teachers already come to Quizizz with a vague idea of the gameplay experience and features, does doubling down on it again provide any additional value? In that case we should double down on the vast library and ease of creation rather than reinforcing the features.</p>
<p>&nbsp;</p>
<p>Maybe if we keep the solution search-first, teachers who know about Quizizz can jump right into action, and the ones who don't know about it will learn about all our features, and that's what we finally did.</p>

# Final solution

<h1>Search-first Personalised Demo Experience</h1>
<p>This is what the final solution looked like:</p>
<p>&nbsp;</p>
<figure class="figure-video align-full" style="padding-bottom:56.206088992974244%"><div><iframe src="https://www.youtube.com/embed/12KJHxotdLc" title="Quizizz Demo - Final" allowfullscreen></iframe></div></figure>
<p>&nbsp;</p>
<p>Let's look at why this worked:</p>
<ul><li>Personalisation at the very start - we started off with building the user's profile.</li><li>Soft start - when teachers answered the initial questions which they knew the answers to, we gained a sort of 'commitment' from the user to complete the journey.</li><li>Personalised journey - by fetching the grade and subject at the very start, we could give them topic recommendations, a personalised demo, and better search results.</li><li>Optional demo - teacher who came to Quizizz pre-informed about the features could skip the demo, while ones who were completely new had an easy way to experience the gameplay.</li><li>Low-commitment actions - instead of making teachers sign up, we made them find value first, explore the platform without any paywalls, and only ask them to register when they try to host an activity.</li><li>Low cognitive load - we kept the onboarding questions simple, gave them trending (and relevant) search terms to choose from, made the demo related to their practice, and had close to zero keyboard usage, hence keeping the cognitive load and hence the effort to a minimum.</li></ul>

# Extras

<h1>Phases</h1>
<p>This project was shipped out in phases. We started out with the very last screen, and then slowly built our way up to the demo and personalisation.</p>
<p>&nbsp;</p>
<h1>Initial feedback</h1>
<p>Teachers loved it. We immediately saw higher activations and higher signup completion rates.</p>
<p>&nbsp;</p>
<p>Apart from boosting activation, we saw an unexpected use case of this feature - an easy way for teachers to pitch Quizizz to their admins. Teachers went with the link in hand and the admins really liked the presentation. This was followed with a boost in leads for the sales team.</p>
<p>&nbsp;</p>
<p>All in all this was a visual-rich, exploration-heavy, rigorous project that I enjoyed!</p>
